# Investigation — "ideal buy point = new" (R8)

**Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Branch:** `worktree-bridge-cse_01ECFeFnu1cGzHZ4G2TgaeNb` (worktree)
**Scope:** investigation only. No production file was modified. All experiments ran from a
throw-away copy of `engine.ts` in `.tmpinvestigate/` (deleted afterwards) that was verified
bit-identical to the shipped engine with all counterfactual switches off
(`max |Δp50| = 2.2e-16` across all 71 vehicles).

**Method.** `buyPointSweep`'s grid was reproduced exactly (`sweepStepMiles` 10,000,
`sweepDraws` 300, seed 42, defaults from `opencawr_data.json`). Baseline reproduces the
reported figures exactly: **argmin at the lowest grid point 62/71, argmin at 0 mi 53/71,
interior optimum 9/71, single-point grid 2/71.**

---

## Verdict (short)

**"Buy new is cheapest per mile" is ~90% an artifact, not a genuine property of the cost
model — and the artifact is a units mismatch in the spec's own $/mi definition, not a bug in
`buypoint.ts`.**

The metric divides **present-value dollars** by **undiscounted miles**. Because
`holdMiles` defaults to `"eol"`, the holding horizon `T` is a *function of the buy odometer*
(19.2 yr at 0 mi vs 10.0 yr at 120k mi for a Corolla). Every recurring cost therefore gets
multiplied by `avgDf(T)`, which falls from 0.73 to 0.56 as `T` grows — so an **identical
physical cost stream is reported as ~23% cheaper for the newer car**. `buyPointSweep` is
faithfully finding the minimum of a metric that is monotone in horizon length.

Two independent neutralizations both collapse the degeneracy, and they are the *same*
mechanism:

| Counterfactual | argmin at grid floor | argmin at 0 mi |
|---|---|---|
| **BASELINE** (`holdMiles:"eol"`, r=7%) | **62/71** | **53/71** |
| H1 `holdMiles` fixed 100k | 7/71 | 1/71 |
| H1 `holdMiles` fixed 50k | 5/71 | 1/71 |
| H1 `holdMiles` fixed 150k | 14/71 | 5/71 |
| H6 PV-consistent denominator (divide by PV of miles) | 9/71 | 3/71 |
| H3 grid floored at first price-curve data point | 61/71 | 0/71 |
| H2 calendar age instead of `buyOdo/annualMiles` | 62/71 | 53/71 |
| r = 5% | 60/71 | 51/71 |
| r = 3% | 58/71 | 49/71 |
| r = 0% | 34/71 | 25/71 |
| H4 price@0 raised +10% | 14/71 | 5/71 |
| H4 price@0 raised +15% | 9/71 | 0/71 |
| ALL (100k hold + PV miles + calendar age + curve floor) | 8/71 | 0/71 |

Fixing `holdMiles` makes `T` identical at every grid point, so `avgDf` becomes a constant and
the artifact vanishes *analytically*. Confirmation from the breakdown: with
`holdMiles: 100_000`, the Corolla's `energy` component is **0.1372 $/mi at 0, 60k and 120k mi
— identical to 4 decimals**. With `"eol"` the same physical energy cost is reported as
**0.0985 / 0.1119 / 0.1280 $/mi**. That 3.0¢/mi spread is pure horizon discounting.

## Ranked causes with quantified contribution

### 1. PV dollars ÷ nominal miles, combined with a buy-odo-dependent horizon — **~90% of the effect**

The decisive measurement. For each vehicle, gap = `p50(odo = lo + 120k) − p50(odo = lo)`
(positive = model prefers the newer car), 2,000 draws, 69 vehicles with a wide enough range:

| | # cars where +120k odo is costlier | mean gap |
|---|---|---|
| nominal denominator (shipped) | **69 / 69** | **+15.88 ¢/mi** |
| PV-consistent denominator | 33 / 69 | **+1.51 ¢/mi** |

**The newness gradient is 15.88 ¢/mi as shipped and 1.51 ¢/mi once the denominator matches the
numerator — 90% of it is the units mismatch.** Under the PV-consistent denominator the sign
*flips* for 36 of 69 cars (older is genuinely cheaper for e.g. Corolla −1.94¢, Camry −2.11¢,
RAV4 −4.89¢, Highlander −7.36¢, Odyssey −9.55¢, XC90 −4.60¢).

Component attribution of the 18.13 ¢/mi mean gap (nominal denominator, `lo` vs `lo+120k`):

| component | mean Δ (old − new) | # cars where old is costlier (of 69) | real or artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| maintenance | +4.79¢ | 69 | real, amplified by the artifact |
| energy | **+3.94¢** | **69** | **100% artifact** — `epm` is a constant per mile |
| repairs | +3.89¢ | 69 | real, amplified |
| depreciation | +2.63¢ | **39** | mixed; *favours the old car in 30/69* |
| insurance | +1.89¢ | 66 | **artifact** — real insurance *falls* with age |
| battery | +0.63¢ | 14 | real |
| useTax | +0.22¢ | 40 | mixed |
| registration | +0.08¢ | 69 | **100% artifact** — flat $/yr |
| tires | 0.00¢ | 0 | undiscounted in the engine; horizon-neutral |
| **total** | **+18.13¢** | 69 | |

Provable-artifact floor (energy + registration + insurance, all flat-or-declining in real
terms) = **5.91 ¢/mi = 33%** of the gap. Under the PV-consistent denominator these three are
essentially constant across buy odometers — Corolla energy 0.1829 / 0.1830 / 0.1831, reg
0.0042 / 0.0042 / 0.0042, insurance 0.1450 / 0.1424 / 0.1414 at 0 / 60k / 120k mi.

**Note the brief's H1 mechanism is inverted.** "Buying at 0 mi spreads fixed costs over 200k
instead of 100k miles" is *not* what happens: depreciation + use tax per mile is **higher**
for the new buy in 30/69 cars and only 2.6¢ lower on average (Corolla 0.1107 @0 vs 0.1075
@120k — the old car is cheaper on depreciation). The conclusion of H1 is right; the driver is
the discount-compression of the **recurring** streams, not the amortization of the fixed ones.

### 2. Price curves back-extrapolated below the observed used market — **the "0 mi" specifically, and a real defect**

`curveAt` extrapolates **both** ends with the edge segment's slope. The docstring cites spec
§2, but §2 only sanctions extrapolation *"past the last data point"* — the **leftward**
extrapolation is undocumented, un-ledgered in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`, and untested
(`packages/core/test/engine.test.ts:68` only covers the right end).

No seed vehicle has a price point below 10,000 mi (the median first point is 15,000 mi;
some are at 30,000). `meta.provenance_note` confirms the curves were anchored to
**used-market** pulls (cars.com / iSeeCars). So the price at 0 mi is always the year-1→year-3
slope projected backwards through the steepest part of the depreciation curve.

**Measured consequence: the modeled year-1 depreciation is 9.3% on average across the 62
in-production vehicles** (mean retention at 13,000 mi = 90.7%), against a widely-cited
real-world ~20%. Worst cases are absurd:

| vehicle | modeled price @0 | @13k mi | implied year-1 depreciation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honda Civic | $25,632 | $24,947 | **2.7%** |
| Toyota Prius (hybrid) | $29,200 | $28,160 | **3.6%** |
| Chevy Suburban | $54,750 | $51,825 | 5.3% |
| Chevy Tahoe | $51,750 | $48,825 | 5.7% |
| Toyota RAV4 Prime | $45,000 | $42,400 | 5.8% |
| Toyota Camry | $29,579 | $27,868 | 5.8% |

How load-bearing is this for the argmin? Very — the sweep is nearly flat near 0. For every one
of the 53 zero-argmin cars I computed the **breakeven uplift**: how much price@0 must rise
before another grid point wins.

- Median breakeven: **+$1,043 (≈3% of the modeled new price)**.
- Range: +$245 (Ford Escape, 1%) to +$2,496 (Chevy Suburban, 5%).
- In **52 of 53 cases the next-best point is 10,000 mi** — the *adjacent* grid point.

Counterfactual (insert a real anchor at odo 0 equal to `p@0 × (1+u)`; `u=0` is a verified
no-op control): **+5% → 26/71 at zero; +10% → 5/71; +15% → 0/71.** A +10% uplift corresponds
to modeled year-1 depreciation of ~17.5%, i.e. still shy of the real ~20%.

**This is a genuine defect and it should be fixed regardless of what R8 decides**, because it
also silently affects `buyPrice`, `useTax`, the `full_cov_threshold_usd` insurance switch and
the total-loss book value for every low-odometer query in the Deal Analyzer and the survey
heatmap — not just `idealOdo`.

Caveat on its power: fixing the price alone moves the median argmin from 0 mi to only
**20,000 mi**, whereas the denominator fix moves it to **70,000 mi**. Price extrapolation
explains the *literal zero*; it does not explain the *newness preference*.

### 3. Feasibility permitting a 0-mile buy — **enabler, not cause**

`feasibleOdoRange`'s lower bound is `max(0, (now_year − last_year) × annualMiles)`, which is
exactly 0 for any model with `last_year >= 2026`. **62/71 seed vehicles have
`last_year = 2026`**, so 62 grids start at 0.

- All **53** zero-argmin cars are current-production (necessarily).
- **9 of the 62** cars whose grid starts at 0 nonetheless have an interior optimum: Prius
  Prime @30k, Corolla @20k, Camry Hybrid @40k, Leaf @30k, Accord @40k, Mazda3 @40k, Model 3
  @50k, ID.4 @30k, Colorado @30k.
- The other 9 of the 62-at-grid-floor are discontinued cars pinned at a *nonzero* floor:
  Bolt @39k, Volt @91k, Fiat 500 @91k, Passat @52k, Sienna V6 @78k, Ranger (old) @195k,
  996 Carrera @208k, 996 Turbo @221k — i.e. **the argmin is at the newest thing that exists,
  whatever odometer that is.** That is the tell: the driver is newness, not the number 0.

Flooring the grid at the first price-curve point confirms it: 0/71 at 0 mi but **61/71 still
at the grid floor**. A used-car odometer floor relabels the degeneracy; it does not remove it.

### 4. Discount rate — **secondary, ~35% of the effect at most**

r=7% → 62/53. r=5% → 60/51. r=3% → 58/49. r=0% → **34/25**. Even at r=0 half the field still
prefers the floor, because with `holdMiles:"eol"` a newer car still buys more remaining life,
and the maintenance/repair curves are odometer-driven. So the `DECISIONS.md` note ("at r=0 the
newest-buy-is-always-cheapest monotonicity no longer holds") is only **partly** true as
implemented — at r=0 it still holds for 34/71.

### 5. Odometer-implied age (`a0 = buyOdo / am`) — **zero contribution at defaults**

`H2` (age = `now_year − deriveBuyYear(...)` instead of `buyOdo/annualMiles`) changes the
counts by **exactly nothing**: 62/71 and 53/71 before and after. This is structural: at the
default 13,000 mi/yr, `deriveBuyYear` is *itself* computed as `now_year − odo/annualMiles`
rounded, so odometer-implied age and calendar age are the same number by construction. They
diverge only (a) when `deriveBuyYear` clamps to `[first_year, last_year]`, and (b) when the
user's `annualMiles` differs from the odometer↔year mapping's own assumption.

It is **not** harmless in general — it just isn't the cause here:

| annual miles | baseline (floor / zero) | with calendar age (floor / zero) | max ∣Δp50∣ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6,000 | 71/71 · 62/71 | 71/71 · 62/71 | 56.1 ¢/mi (996 Carrera @0) |
| 13,000 | 62/71 · 53/71 | 62/71 · 53/71 | 38.6 ¢/mi (996 Carrera @0) |
| 20,000 | 48/71 · 40/71 | **31/71 · 23/71** | 28.8 ¢/mi (996 Carrera @0) |

So it *does* bite at 20,000 mi/yr (removes 17 more floor-argmins) and the per-car $/mi error
reaches 38.6¢ even at defaults, for discontinued cars where the year clamp fires. Spec §10
already flags this; the measurement above is new and should go in the ledger.

Note also the low-mileage row: at 6,000 mi/yr the degeneracy is **total (71/71)** — exactly
what cause #1 predicts, since `T` blows up to ~33 years and `avgDf` collapses.

## The answer to the owner's question

> "ideal = new seems wrong, or one of our assumptions could be driving it to be wrong."

Both, in this order:

1. **The dominant driver is a definitional one, and it is in the spec, not in `buypoint.ts`.**
   Spec §2 defines `$/mi = PV(costs) / (sell_odo − buy_odo)`. A PV numerator over a nominal
   denominator is only comparable between two options with the **same horizon**. `holdMiles:"eol"`
   guarantees they never do. Spec §5's "the newest car is always cheapest per mile" was
   diagnosed as an *opportunity-cost truth*; it is actually a **units artifact** — 90% of it
   survives at r=3% and half of it survives at r=0%, which an opportunity-cost effect would not.
2. **Underneath it there is a real data defect** (leftward price extrapolation → 9.3% modeled
   year-1 depreciation) that pins the argmin at *literally* 0 rather than merely "newish", and
   that corrupts price, use tax, insurance tier and total-loss book value everywhere else too.

So R8's premise — "this is structural, not a bug, needs a redefinition of *ideal*" — is
**half right**. A redefinition of `idealOdo` is still needed (the model has a legitimate
preference for newer, worth ~1.5¢/mi, and reporting a single argmin over a near-flat curve is
poor UX either way). But the engine also has two fixable problems, and after fixing them the
"ideal" is a real interior optimum for 60+ of 71 cars, which makes most of the redefinition
candidates unnecessary.

## Proposed minimal fixes (NOT implemented)

**F1 — price curve: stop extrapolating leftward past the first data point.** *(smallest,
highest-confidence, do this one first)*
`packages/core/src/curves.ts:26-28`. Two options:
- (a) **clamp** below the first point (`x <= first.x → first.y`) — matches what
  `maintenanceAt` already does at its low end, is one line, and makes the model refuse to
  invent a new-car price it has no data for; or
- (b) require a real **odo-0 anchor** per vehicle in `price_vs_odometer_usd` (correct, but it
  is a data-sourcing project — 62 new-car transaction prices).
(a) is the minimal fix; it makes the sub-first-point region flat-but-honest rather than
confidently wrong, and it forces the buy-point grid to be floored at the first data point
(itself a defensible "used-car floor" for R8). Either way, add a ledger row: leftward
extrapolation is currently undocumented in both `OpenCAWR_SPEC.md` §2 and `ASSUMPTIONS.md` §B.
**Caution:** (a) is *not* conservative in the buy-new direction — it makes new cars look
*cheaper* still. It must ship with a grid floor (`lo = max(lo, first curve x)`) or with (b).

**F2 — make the $/mi denominator match its numerator.** *(the real fix; a model revision,
needs owner sign-off)*
`packages/core/src/engine.ts:178`. Divide by `Σ_t w·annualMiles·(1+r)^-t` instead of `miles`,
and fold energy in as PV dollars rather than adding `epm × avgDf` on the outside. This makes
$/mi horizon-invariant for a constant real cost stream, which is what makes cross-horizon and
cross-buy-point comparison legitimate at all. Equivalent framings the owner may prefer:
report **equivalent annual cost ÷ annual miles**, or keep the current number but only ever
compare buy points at a **fixed `holdMiles`**.

**F3 — if F2 is rejected, constrain the sweep instead.** Have `buyPointSweep` run at a fixed
`holdMiles` (the user's chosen horizon, defaulting to something like 100k) rather than
`"eol"`, so every grid point shares one horizon. This is a `buypoint.ts`-only change, needs no
engine revision, does not move any reference output, and by itself takes 62/71 → 7/71 and
53/71 → 1/71. **If the owner wants one change that fixes R8 without a numbers-change event,
this is it.**

**F4 — carry real model year as engine state** (spec §10, already known). Worth doing for the
20,000 mi/yr and discontinued-car cases quantified above; irrelevant to R8 at defaults.

## Suggested order

F3 (or F1+grid floor) unblocks R8 today with zero reference-output churn. F1 is a correctness
fix worth doing on its own merits and is cheap. F2 is the honest answer to "is buy-new really
cheapest" and should be run past the owner as a model revision, with regenerated reference
outputs, exactly as `DECISIONS.md` prescribes for spec §10 revisions.

## Ledger rows this investigation should add to `ASSUMPTIONS.md`

1. `curveAt` extrapolates **leftward** past the first price point with the first segment's
   slope — undocumented in spec §2 (which sanctions only the right end), untested, and it
   implies a mean year-1 depreciation of **9.3%** across the 62 in-production seed vehicles
   (Civic 2.7%, Prius 3.6%) against a real-world ~20%.
2. The `$/mi` metric is **not horizon-invariant**: PV numerator, nominal denominator. A
   constant real cost stream is reported ~23% cheaper at a 19-year horizon than at a 10-year
   one. This, not opportunity cost, is what produces spec §5's monotonicity — measured at
   15.88 ¢/mi nominal vs 1.51 ¢/mi PV-consistent across 69 vehicles.
3. Odometer-implied age is **identical** to calendar age at the default 13,000 mi/yr (both
   derive from `odo / annualMiles`); it diverges only under the `deriveBuyYear` production
   clamp or at non-default `annualMiles` — measured max 38.6 ¢/mi at defaults, and it removes
   17 floor-argmins at 20,000 mi/yr.
