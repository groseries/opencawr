# OpenCAWR — Assumptions Ledger

The single written record of every assumption in the model: explicit, implicit, inferred,
and contested. **Owner directive (2026-07-27): the 71 seed `model_output` rows from the
lost prototype are a REFERENCE, not truth.** On 2026-07-27 the owner made the v2b model
(as documented in the prototype's own `CAWR.xlsx` "Assumptions"/"Model & Method" tabs)
canonical, and the reference outputs were **regenerated from our engine**; the prototype's
originals are preserved per-vehicle as `model_output_prototype` (revision-log ethos: keep
old numbers, never delete). Update this file whenever an assumption is added, changed,
generalized, or retired. Statuses:

- **SOURCED** — traceable to a real dataset/publication
- **DOCUMENTED** — stated in the prototype's own docs (CAWR.xlsx tabs)
- **JUDGMENT** — a deliberate, labeled modeling choice with no external source
- **USER-SPECIFIC** — true for the owner's situation only; must become a user input before general release

## B. Model structure (v2b, canonical since 2026-07-27 — all implemented in `@opencawr/core`)

| Mechanism | Form | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Cost equation | [dep + use tax + Σ disc(maint, ins, reg, total-loss, repairs) + tires + battery] ÷ miles + energy × avg discount factor | DOCUMENTED |
| Depreciation | price curve at buy odo → resale (scrap at EOL; curve value at earlier sell), extrapolated, floored at scrap | DOCUMENTED |
| Use tax | rate × purchase price at t0 | DOCUMENTED |
| Total-loss exposure | 1.5%/yr × $750 deductible while full-coverage; × full book value when liability-only. Does NOT truncate life (open limitation) | DOCUMENTED |
| Insurance | per-model premium (pre-multiplier in data) × multiplier; liability-only once book < $6k; noise Normal(1, 0.08) per year | DOCUMENTED |
| Major-repair tail | Poisson past 120k mi, hazard ×(1+(odo−120k)/100k); cost lognormal σ=0.5 × make-mult × year-mult + $600 hassle | DOCUMENTED |
| Calendar-age escalator | ×(1 + 2%/yr past age 8) on scheduled maintenance AND repair-event costs (doc says "repairs"; broad reading is a JUDGMENT). Age = odo ÷ annual miles — real calendar age still open (worst for garage-kept/passion cars) | DOCUMENTED + JUDGMENT |
| Year-reliability multiplier | landmine ×1.40 / caution ×1.15 / sweet-spot ×0.95, repair costs only | DOCUMENTED |
| Battery | per-vehicle data fields (prob, pack $, σ): EV 30%×$12k, Tesla 30%×$14k, Leaf 55%×$8k, PHEV 22%×$4k, hybrid 15%×$2.5k; Bernoulli × lognormal at 65% of life | DOCUMENTED (values are labeled judgment) |
| Energy | outside the Monte Carlo (deterministic horizon); EV kWh ×1.08 degradation; elec ×1.09 EV / ×1.00 PHEV DCFC premium (R7: was ×1.06); 0%/yr price escalation. **Energy sits entirely outside the Monte Carlo loop — fuel-price and efficiency risk contribute zero variance to P50/P75/P90**; every draw for a given car sees the same deterministic `energy` term. Accepted, documented v2b limitation, deferred by owner decision 2026-07-28 (see §E) | DOCUMENTED |
| PHEV utility factor (R7, mileage-dependent, anchored) | `uf = clamp(uf_seed × ufRaw(R_eff, annualMiles) / ufRaw(R, constants.annual_miles), 0, 1)`, where `ufRaw(R, am) = 1 − exp(−R / (am/365))` models the share of daily miles falling within electric range `R`, treating daily distance as exponential with mean `am/365`. **This is a J2841-shaped approximation, not J2841 itself** — SAE J2841 derives its utility-factor curves from a real multi-day travel-survey distribution, not a single exponential, and no J2841 table or coefficient is used or cited here; the closed form only supplies the *sensitivity* of uf to annual mileage. The anchor (dividing by `ufRaw` at the vehicle's own `R` and the baseline `constants.annual_miles`) pins the *curve* to each vehicle's seed `phev_utility_factor`, so the closed form contributes only the response to mileage away from baseline, never a recalibration of the car's absolute electric share. **It does not reproduce the seed uf exactly in production**: the numerator uses the degraded range `R_eff = R / ev_kwh_degradation_mult` while the denominator uses undegraded `R`, so at baseline mileage `uf = uf_seed × ufRaw(R_eff)/ufRaw(R)`, which is slightly *below* `uf_seed` — Volt at 13,000 mi/yr comes out 0.7728 against a seed of 0.80. That gap is the degradation effect and is intended (see the PHEV degradation row below); the exact-anchor identity holds only with degradation disabled, which is how the unit tests isolate it. Falls back to the fixed seed `phev_utility_factor` (no mileage sensitivity) when a vehicle has no `electric_range_mi` (e.g. a future pipeline-assembled PHEV that hasn't been given one). Sanity check at 13,000 mi/yr (pre-anchor, undegraded `ufRaw` vs. seed uf): Volt 0.774 vs 0.80, RAV4 Prime 0.693 vs 0.75, Prius Prime 0.505 vs 0.55, Pacifica 0.593 vs 0.45 — three of four land within ~9%, evidence the exponential shape is a reasonable fit; Pacifica is the outlier the anchor absorbs | JUDGMENT |
| PHEV `electric_range_mi` (R7, new optional `VehicleSpecs` field, EPA-sourced) | Chevy Volt 53 mi (2016-2019 model years, all Gen 2 — the seed row's `first_year`/`last_year` span only Gen 2; Gen 1, 2011-2015 at 38 mi, isn't represented in this row at all); Toyota RAV4 Prime 42 mi (single generation since its 2021 launch, no boundary to resolve); Toyota Prius Prime 25 mi (the seed row's `last_year`=2026 nominally spans the 2023 redesign to 44 mi, but `pinned_buy_year_est`=2019 and the existing `kwh_per_100mi`=25 both land squarely in Gen 1 (2017-2022) — used Gen 1's 25 mi, not the newer 44 mi, so the two fields stay internally consistent); Chrysler Pacifica PHEV 32 mi (no full redesign in this window, but EPA's own certified range drifted 33 mi (2017-2018) → 32 mi (2020+) within the same generation; `pinned_buy_year_est`=2021 anchors to 32 mi — note the seed's pre-existing `kwh_per_100mi`=40 actually traces to the earlier 2017-2018 certification (41 kWh/100mi from 2020 on), a small pre-existing cross-year inconsistency in the seed data, disclosed here rather than silently reconciled since fixing it is outside this task's scope). All four verified directly against fueleconomy.gov EPA comparison pages, 2026-07-28 | SOURCED |
| PHEV pack degradation (R7) | Reuses `constants.ev_kwh_degradation_mult` (1.08) — no new constant. Applied both ways: `kwh_per_100mi × 1.08` (raises electric-mode consumption) and `R_eff = electric_range_mi / 1.08` (shrinks the effective range fed into the utility-factor calc above, which lowers uf and shifts miles to gas). Same fixed mid-life multiplier EVs already carry throughout their holding period — not a function of holding period or vehicle age | DOCUMENTED |
| PHEV DCFC premium (R7) | `constants.dcfc_elec_mult_phev` 1.06 → 1.00. PHEVs charge overnight at home (unlike the EV assumption baking in ~15% DCFC use) and several PHEV models can't DC fast charge at all, so the EV-style DCFC premium doesn't apply. Left in the data at 1.00 rather than deleted, so the decision stays visible and editable | OWNER DECISION |
| PHEV `kwh_per_100mi` convention | All four PHEV rows (Volt 31, Prius Prime 25, RAV4 Prime 36, Pacifica 40) are EPA **electric-mode** (charge-depleting) consumption, confirmed directly against fueleconomy.gov, not the blended combined-electric+gas MPGe figure — a blended figure would double-count that vehicle's own gas portion once multiplied by `uf` in `energyPerMile`. Any future pipeline-assembled PHEV must supply the electric-mode figure, never blended, or it silently reintroduces this bug | SOURCED |
| EOL | `eol_maintained_miles` = iSeeCars empirical × 1.30 "maintained" bonus (baked in); lognormal dispersion per tier (§C) | SOURCED × JUDGMENT |
| Opportunity cost | ONLY via discounting; +5%/yr capital charge proven double-counting, removed v2b | DOCUMENTED |
| Sport/passion vehicles | NO special-case rule (owner decision): a vehicle of passion runs through the same engine with its own inputs (low annual miles, chosen horizon, curve-based resale). The calendar-age limitation bites hardest here | OWNER DECISION |
| Monte Carlo | N=1,100 default, seed 42, own deterministic PRNG; P50 stability ~±$0.004 across seeds | DOCUMENTED |
| Buy-point sweep (R4, `packages/core/src/buypoint.ts`) | Grid search over `costPerMile`'s own `buyOdo` input across `feasibleOdoRange` (reused from `feasibility.ts`, not re-derived), clamped ≥0 and capped at `eol_maintained_miles`, step `sweepStepMiles`=10,000 mi; `idealOdo`/`idealYear` = the grid point minimizing P50. Adds no new pricing logic — `costPerMile` itself is unchanged. Two low-production, narrow-window cars (Porsche 996 Carrera/Turbo) hit an edge case at high annual-mileage inputs where the year-implied lower bound exceeds the `eol_maintained_miles` cap; the range collapses to the single capped point rather than inverting | JUDGMENT |
| Buy-point sweep draws | `sweepDraws`=300/point (vs. the 1,100-draw rail default), at a fixed seed so every grid point is directly comparable — the survey drawer's 400-draw reduction (§I) is the existing precedent for this speed/precision tradeoff on a many-point grid search | JUDGMENT |
| Buy-point sweep "worthwhile" tolerance | `worthwhileP50Tolerance`=5% of the sweep's cheapest (`idealP50`) — defines "still worth buying" for the upper mileage limit | JUDGMENT |
| Buy-point sweep upper-limit walk (contiguity) | Walking upward from `idealOdo`, take the last odometer within tolerance, stopping at the FIRST point that exceeds it — a later grid point that dips back under tolerance past a violation is excluded, so the reported range can't jump a gap. `upperOdo` is `null` when the walk can't take a single step (`idealOdo` is the grid's last point, or the very next point already violates) | JUDGMENT |

## D. Data-quality flags (seed set)

- **`model_output` is generated by our engine** (meta.reference_engine records draws/seed/date); `model_output_prototype` is the lost prototype's original — historical only, never asserted.
- **Porsche 996 Carrera / Turbo**: prototype rows used a special sport rule (70k-mi hold, resale ≈ purchase). Our reference computes them drive-to-death like everything else — their headline $/mi is not meaningful for a passion-use case; use per-car inputs instead.
- **Fiat 500**: all model years marked `bad` (v2b audit: "no 500 model year is reliable") — encoded in data, not code.
- **Maintenance curves shared** across 43 cars (`maintenance_curve_shared_with` field) — per-car precision is an illusion; fine for tiers, not head-to-head claims.
- **Kia K4** is `proxied` (no used history); inherits every proxy assumption.
- **Reliability tiers + per-make multipliers are judgment calls** (xlsx Part 4c), and reliability data traces to Consumer Reports → **public-launch blocked** until re-derived from NHTSA/CarComplaints/RepairPal (spec §9).
- The prototype's Any-Car Calculator used a *simpler second model* — never port it; one engine only.
